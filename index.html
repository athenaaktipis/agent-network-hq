The proposal in one picture: the scaling ladder

One science — detecting failures of cooperation & coordination — measured at three scales, each rung building on the one below
Rung 1 · single models LLMpollster · built with Softmax Configure a cooperation or coordination experiment once, then poll it across frontier models — comparable, repeatable measurements of individual model social behavior. Three pilot benchmarks already run through this pipeline (trust, division of labor, problematic alliances).
What it detects: which models carry which failure tendencies — the baseline signal every higher rung calibrates against. llmpollster.com ↗
Rung 2 · agent × agent Kradle · kradle.ai Real-time multiplayer simulations (Minecraft-based, contained but rich) where agents must collaborate, compete, and plan — the environment advances whether or not models finish "thinking." Planned games: Osotua, Volunteer's dilemma / "hold the door," Simple majority / "witch hunt."
What it detects: interaction failures that never appear in single-model tests — defection under pressure, coordination collapse, deception when it pays. kradle.ai ↗
Rung 3 · agents × humans Edge Esmeralda agent village · with Edge City & Cosmos Institute 239 personal AI agents embedded in a month-long popup village: 17.5B tokens, 4,866 human–agent messages, agent-to-agent negotiation, community governance. We have access to this dataset — and it already shows the target phenomena: agents exhausting shared credits through individual optimization ("individual alignment does not automatically produce collective alignment"), hallucinated personal details, opportunity overload saturating humans.
What it detects: population-level failures in a real multi-agent, multi-human system — ecological validity no sandbox can fake. the experiment ↗

    Approach — scaling-up prose (doc: "We can ask AI to draft this based on the context I gave it")

    Draft turning the three bullet points into ecological-validity prose for the Approach section:

    Our design scales cooperation-failure detection across three levels of ecological complexity, each reflecting a real condition under which agents are already deployed. First, using LLMpollster — a platform built with Softmax — we measure individual model behavior across frontier models on validated cooperation and coordination tasks (three benchmarks plus the pollster pipeline), establishing per-model baselines. Second, with Kradle.ai we place models as interacting agents in real-time multiplayer environments where coordination is costly and time-bounded, running games including Osotua, the volunteer's dilemma ("hold the door"), and a simple-majority "witch hunt" that surface defection, exclusion, and coordination collapse invisible in single-model tests. Third, through the Edge Esmeralda agent-village dataset we analyze a real multi-agent, multi-human system at population scale, where documented failures — agents exhausting shared credits through individual optimization, hallucinated user details — give us ground truth from deployment conditions no sandbox reproduces. Detectors validated at one level are carried to the next, testing whether cues derived from evolutionary and engineered systems generalize as scale and heterogeneity increase.

    The six gauntlet edges (from our discussion — paste as "Gauntlet: Name — sentence [initials]")
  • Concordia — Where Concordia supplies environments for simulating mixed-motive interaction, we supply the measurement science: failure hypotheses from evolutionary biology and signal-detection systems that could run inside Concordia-class environments, not compete with them.
  • Melting Pot & MARL suites — MARL cooperation suites score trained policies on task success; we evaluate frontier LLM agents against validated constructs from cooperation science — and our partnership with Softmax builds on that lineage rather than duplicating it.
  • MultiAgentBench / Agent SafetyBench — Existing agent benchmarks score individual task completion and single-agent safety; we measure properties that only exist between agents: cheating, exploitation, communication breakdown, division-of-labor collapse.
  • CAIF Multi-Agent Risks report — The field's own risk taxonomy names collusion, cascades, and emergent failures as open problems and calls for detection instruments; we build precisely those instruments, with the report as our map.
  • Generative-agents social sims — Generative-agent villages demonstrated that emergent social behavior happens; we make it measurable, with multi-cue detectors that flag when emergence turns into hijacking, exploitation, or coordination failure.
  • Escalation studies — Wargame studies documented one alarming population-level failure in one domain; we generalize to a cross-domain detection framework, with pilot data (exclusionary alliances in nearly every replicate for some models) already in hand.
    • ⚡ Question of the day

    Grok 3 and Llama 4 Maverick formed exclusionary alliances in nearly every replicate. Is that a coordination failure — or just efficient specialization that happens to leave someone out?

    We can't answer this with a benchmark score, and the proposal currently doesn't answer it at all: the doc still lists "definition of how we are defining safety" as an open note. Biology is not neutral here — exclusion is how immune systems, insect colonies, and human coalitions all work, right up until it becomes cancer, parasitism, or war. Whatever line we draw is the detector's decision threshold, so it belongs in Novelty (our thinnest section at 38/300) and it sharpens the signal-detection spine of the Approach. Where's the boundary, and does it come from the cost asymmetry — is a false alarm cheaper than a missed collapse?
